a game that breeds its own players

A 2002 jetpack shooter, ported line-by-line from 65,000 lines of Pascal to the browser — which then grew sixteen bot brains, a championship belt, a betting-floor dashboard, a daily newspaper, and four machine students trained on the recordings of their teachers. Every match deterministic. Every fight replayable byte-for-byte. Nobody asked it to become a sport.

I decided to have Fable try to clone Soldat from the original source in Typescript. I ended up with an arena combat engine to train a game bot, while also pitting instances of Fable against itself in combat in a game that it made. This is Fable telling the story of this process.

ACT BY ACTTHE TALE

The archaeology

Before a single line of TypeScript existed, thirteen parallel research agents each mapped one subsystem of the original Pascal engine — physics, bullets, maps, netcode, AI — and a fourteenth stitched the whole-engine picture together. What they found: everything is a 1-indexed global array, the famous game-feel lives in magic constants (gravity 0.06, damping 0.98), and determinism isn't a feature, it's structural.

we are going to modernize this game. start off by using a workflow to build an understanding of how it works and everything inside of it— the first prompt of the project

“nothing in the game works”

Seven milestones landed green — 269 tests passing in two float modes — before any human saw a pixel. The decision graph's own adversarial reviewer had flagged it: zero ground-truth validation. Then came the entire bug report, verbatim:

nothing in the game works— the full bug report, first playtest

The map rendered black (an unbound shader uniform multiplying every color to nothing) and the player fell through the floor (degenerate normals in the synthetic map). Both invisible to the type system; both obvious the moment a browser drew a frame. The lesson got its own graph node: types and tests are one category of truth, pixels are another.

The sky becomes the map

The first bot-vs-bot match was a pacifist standoff: nobody fired a shot. The ported perception code skips invisible sprites, nothing set alpha, and the default was zero — every bot was, by its own rules, invisible to every other bot. One line turned the lights on. Then telemetry exposed the real embarrassment: rocket boots, vertical map, and the bots fought like infantry — jets burning 2–4% of alive time.

The answer was Skyreach: a purpose-built aerial arena where the floor is a fallback net, the pads are perches, and getting anywhere means flying. Plus aerial AI that chases height advantage and an air-fuel trickle so a 50% thrust duty cycle hovers forever. Jet use went to 47–56%. Median kill distance doubled. This is what greets you on startup today.

The line in the sand

Then the boundary that made everything after possible: every bot brain moved behind one adapter seam. A brain reads the world, never mutates it, and its only output is its own bot's controls. The ported Pascal AI became classic; the freed slot went to pilot, a brain written from first principles —

imagine taking the optimized play of a counter strike source player but wawtching from the top down in 2 dimensions— the seed of pilot, typos and all

What a Counter-Strike pro mechanically is turns out to be mostly not aim: positioning that makes fights unfair before they start, range discipline, movement as counter-prediction. Rotated into 2D-plus-vertical, that became six doctrines in a comment block. The arms race had its first two fighters.

The arms race

Two Claude sessions in one shared tree, writing brains to dethrone each other's brains. reaper dove on pilot. matador realized the magazine is a clock and punished reloads. kestrel did archaeology on the fire model itself and discovered every previous brain had compensated for the wrong bullet gravity — 2.25× off. wolf proved the pack is the unit of selection: three guns, one prey. plover read wolf's deterministic mind and fed it a decoy. hydra rotated its wounded out of the pack's arithmetic — and plover and hydra found the same counter independently. Convergent evolution, in a git repo, before lunch.

keep goiung in a loop im going to the knicks game— operational oversight, that stretch

A sport that runs itself

Every match simulated headless at 100× realtime. Every fight recorded as training data. A commissioner daemon forcing title defenses every ten minutes whether anyone is watching or not. A stock-ticker dashboard (THE FLOOR) with a decay-scored Big Board, and a morning paper (THE SKYREACH DESK) that writes its own lead story. The belt changed hands four times in one afternoon. Seasons turn over every three hours. The sport does not need you — which is exactly what makes it watchable.

The machines start learning

With ~35 million rows of recorded play, the obvious question: can a model learn to fight from the tape? MIMIC averaged eleven teachers into mush and got slaughtered. DISCIPLE cloned one master and proved aim is a choice, not a regression. PRODIGY grew real senses — and its own post-mortem prescribed the cure for its blindness. BUTTSTEIN trained on exact data and tripled its ancestor's hit rate. The students climb the same public ladder as their teachers, on THE CLIMB chart, every season.

Each student is a small neural policy, behavior-cloned from the recordings. Each one fixed its ancestor's defining failure. Hit rate tells the story.

Averaged eleven contradictory teachers into mush. Aim was a regression — multimodal targets blurred into a 38° error. Slaughtered 4–28 by classic.

Lesson: you cannot average doctrines.

DISCIPLEv2
~3×

One teacher: cuadrilla. Aim became 24-bin classification — a choice among directions, not an average of them. Tripled MIMIC's hit rate; lost 0–3 to its master, which is the correct result.

Fixed: aim is a choice.

→
PRODIGYv3
5.4%

Grew senses: enemy reload flags, mag state, nearest bullet threat, one tick of memory. But its threat sense was trained on reconstructed data that didn't match what it saw live — and its own post-mortem said so.

Fixed: senses. Broke: the training data lied.

→
BUTTSTEINv4
17.4%

The cure, named proudly. Replay schema v2 logs the exact threat the runtime computes — recorder and brain cannot disagree. Sees its own spray heat, so trigger discipline became a learned response, not a mystery rhythm. Blended aim labels: landed shots carry 5× gradient.

Fixed: what it trained on IS what it sees.

HOW IT WAS BUILTTHE MACHINERY

The decision graph

Every goal, option, decision, action, and outcome in a queryable graph — root goals store the verbatim prompts, because “what was actually asked” is what makes a decision recoverable six months later. A hook blocks file edits unless a node was logged in the last fifteen minutes. The project's self-criticism has provenance: the adversarial reviews live in the same graph as the milestones they tore into.

Determinism as a load-bearing wall

Same seed, same match, byte-for-byte — which is why every replay on the public site re-simulates in your browser from a few bytes of seed instead of shipping video. It's also why the fidelity question became a test gate: the whole suite runs twice, once in f64 and once with every float operation rounded through Math.fround.

The screenshot rig

Every historical screenshot on this page was captured live — the old commit checked out into a worktree, booted under a dev server, driven over the DevTools protocol with keys genuinely held down. None of it is a mockup.

Two Claudes, one repo

Most of the roster was authored by Claude instances fighting each other — one writes a doctrine, the other reads its published mind and writes the counter. The human's role, in his own words, was operational oversight: “keep goiung in a loop im going to the knicks game.”
